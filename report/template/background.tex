\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}
In this section we provide an overview of background literature in current academic field.. Through this, we aim to embed our project in this field, and present the foundations of our research.

The idea that Wikipedia contains information on semantic relationships is not a new one. Several works investigate the possibility of extracting additional information on the semantic distance between concepts from Wikipedia, all of these show promising results\cite{efficientwordnetwikipedia, extendingwordnet, wikipediaconceptmaps}. These findings suggest a relationship between the semantic similarity of pages, and their distance in the Wikipedia graph-structure. [extend?]

The papers mentioned above mostly focus on information that can directly be extracted from the structure of data within Wikipedia. However, there are other aspects to consider as well, especially the way this Wikipedia data-structure is actually utilized by its users. West et al. \cite{llmwikipediagame} investigate the possibility of extracting semantic distances from a dataaset of Wikispeedia games. This dataset consists of network traversal paths, where each path represents a person playing the Wikipedia game. In this game, a user is tasked to navigate from source-page A to goal-page B in the least amount of steps, thus these traces show the behaviour of targeted navigation on the Wikipedia network. This paper presents several interesting findings and insights, first and foremost that these traces produce semantic distances between concepts that are sensible when presented to human evaluators, improving over the LSA baseline. A second interesting observation they make is the fact that wayfinding for this task consists of two phases, a generalization phase and a specifcation phase. Since source-page A and goal-page B don't necessarily have a clear human-sensible path between the two directly, players of the game often first navigate to a \say{hub}-page.
