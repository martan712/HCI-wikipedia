We present our results in \Cref{fig:qbin-length} and \Cref{fig:bin-length}. These figures differ in the binning strategy used: \Cref{fig:qbin-length} uses proportional binning and \Cref{fig:bin-length} uses fixed size binning. We will go over the various results we have, grouping them by the used distance metric.

\paragraph{Semantic distance}
We found that the semantic distance by itself is a relatively poor indicator of task difficulty, with $r = 0.56$ and $r = 0.70$. However, by including the PageRank of the target article, results improved significantly, going up to $r = 0.84$ and even $r = 0.89$. We believe the reason for this is that the semantic distance by itself does not take into account the graph structure, which seems to be an important factor in total task difficulty. Adding PageRank ensures both of these are factored in.

\paragraph{Semantic graph distance}
The results here are good, giving $r = 0.91$ for proportional binning and $r = 0.79$ for fixed-size binning without PageRank. This further reaffirms our earlier belief that graph structure is important: this distance metric includes the graph structure by definition. Adding PageRank gives us $r = 0.87$ for both binning methods, which is a relatively small difference, giving a small improvement for one binning type but a small regression for the other. Overall it seems that adding PageRank here thus does not contribute much.

\paragraph{Graph distance}
These results are very limited when we do not include PageRank, as they collapse into 5 or 6 bins. This is because there are only very few possible values here. As such the r-values of $r = 0.99$ and $r = 0.97$ look nice, but are kind of `inflated' by the low number of datapoints. However, it is interesting to see that the relationship does seem to hold according to the limited information we have.

When we add PageRank, which gives us a reasonable number of bins again, we find $r = 0.80$ and $r = 0.81$. This is lower than the other two distance metrics, which seems to indicate that the semantic distance adds useful information about the difficulty of a task.

\paragraph{No distance}
Finally, using only PageRank as a difficulty metric gives mediocre results, with $r = 0.74$ and $r = 0.73$. Given that this metric only looks at the target article and not the source article, we think it would be unreasonable to expect better; in fact it is worse than all other metrics we tried, other than semantic distance which ignores the graph structure completely.

Of course, there are no results for not using a distance metric and also ignoring PageRank, as we then do not have a difficulty metric at all. This explains the empty graphs in our figures.
